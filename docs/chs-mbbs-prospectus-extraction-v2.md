1. Programme and governance
 
Degree: MB.BS. Minimum 6 years (12 semesters) for UTME entrants and 5 years (10 semesters) for Direct Entry (three A'Level passes).
Entry routes (p. 24): UTME, Direct Entry and Special Entry (intra- or inter-university transfer with CGPA ≥ 3.50). §1.1 has the details.
Graduation: at least 160 CU. All courses are compulsory, and no carry-over is allowed except GST and EPS courses.
Classification: the degree is unclassified.
Distinction: awarded for ≥ 70% in a subject, and shown on the certificate.
MBBS Honours: awarded for at least one distinction in each of the four Professional exams.
Governance:
The College Academic Board is the highest academic decision-making body, subject to final approval by the BSU Senate. Benue State Teaching Hospital (BSTH) is represented on it.
100L results go from the Chief Examiner, through the Dean of Science, to the Provost, CHS. An examiners' meeting is held at the end of each session.
Effective date of the regulations: Wednesday, 29 September 2021. They apply to all Professional exams from that date.
Phases:
Pre-Medical (100L)
Pre-clinical (200L–300L, taught over three semesters)
Clinical: "Years 4, 5 and 6", starting after the student passes the 1st Professional MBBS. Clinical clerkship lasts 152–156 weeks in total. The clinical years are split into Blocks, and each Block is subdivided into Junior and Senior Postings (p. 96).
 
1.1 Entry requirements (p. 25)
O'Level: credit passes in 5 subjects (English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology), in no more than 2 sittings.
UTME subjects: English, Physics, Biology and Chemistry, plus the University's cut-off score and screening.
Direct Entry: either
3 A'Level passes (at least 10 points) in Physics, Chemistry and Biology/Zoology in one sitting; or
a Second Class Upper degree or better in a relevant science, plus the O'Level credits.
Special admission: transfer with CGPA ≥ 3.50.

5.1 College-wide rule
CA 30% and Professional Exam 70% at every stage.
Pass mark: ≥ 50% per subject. For clinical subjects (3rd and 4th exams), the student also needs ≥ 50% in the clinical component (CA and exam together).
Clinical CA sources: periodic evaluation by clinical supervisors, plus end-of-posting examinations.

6. Other useful facts
 
100L exam mechanics:
Each course has a panel of examiners, with the Head of Department as Chief Examiner.
The panel sets, moderates and marks the papers.
Each paper runs 1–3 hours.
Paediatrics results are released within one week of the exam.
Clinical teaching modes:
bedside teaching; didactic lectures and tutorials
ward rounds and theatre sessions; outpatient clinics; emergency procedures
laboratory and post-mortem practice; nursing services; rehabilitation
community health programmes; hospital administration
Clinical subject groupings (p. 95):
Medicine: General Medicine and its sub-specialties, plus Psychiatry.
Paediatrics.
Surgery: General Surgery, Anaesthesia, ENT, Orthopaedics, Ophthalmology, Neurosurgery, Radiology.
O&G.
Pathology: Microbiology & Parasitology, Chemical Pathology & Immunology, Haematology, Morbid Anatomy & Forensic Medicine.
Community Health: Biostatistics, Epidemiology, PHC & Community Medicine.
MMP units: Bacteriology, Virology, Parasitology, Immunology and Mycology. Each is headed by a consultant.
Syllabus depth: full topic lists exist for every course. They are not reproduced here, but I can extract them into a course catalogue if you want one.

8. Conflicts and gaps to resolve with the College
 
1. Pharmacology CA weight. The Pharmacology chapter says CA is 50% of the exam mark, but the regulations say 30% everywhere.
2. Surgery attendance. Surgery requires 80%, but the regulations say 70% for clinical stages.
3. Surgery duration. The stated total is 30 weeks, but the listed phases add up to 26 weeks (S0–S3) or 34 weeks (with the SUG 606 revision block).
4. O&G exam grouping. O&G says its Professional exam is held "together with Community Health and Paediatrics". The regulations put Community Medicine in the 4th exam, not the 3rd.
5. Community Medicine exam levels. The Community Medicine chapter says its Professional exams are at 300 and 600 levels. The regulations list it only in the 4th exam.
6. Pharmacology timing. Pharmacology mentions a "revision tutorial session in the 5th year" and calls its exam "Part II", yet the exam falls at the end of 400L. PHT III's level is not stated.
7. Surgery CA reference. Surgery refers to "the final 5th MB.BS examination". There are only four Professional exams.
8. Final-exam wording. The 4th Professional exam rules say "one or both subjects" although three subjects are listed. They also point back to the "Third Professional" exam and to "500 Level" CA, which look like copy-paste errors.
9. 100L credit totals. The course table sums to 45 CU (Physics = 10 CU), but the text says 46 CU (Physics = 11 CU).
10. Chemical Pathology levels. The course description says 400/500 level, but the course codes and posting table say 300/400.
